Demand-Based Automated Water Distribution System

University of Agriculture, Faisalabad · Final Year Project Track 1

A smart distribution system that regulates water flow from real-time environmental demand, so water reaches the lines when it is needed and is not wasted when it is not.

  1. SenseEnvironmental sensors report real-time demand.
  2. DecideAn Arduino microcontroller turns each reading into a flow decision.
  3. ActActuators open and close motorized valves to match.
  4. DeliverWater reaches the distribution lines with less waste.

Feedback returns the valve response to the controller, which keeps sensor-to-valve timing accurate.

  • Built a microcontroller system that adjusts flow as conditions change.
  • Integrated automated actuators and motorized valves to trigger the physical components and cut water waste.
  • Developed local control loops and feedback mechanisms for reliable network performance.

AI-Powered UAV System for Weed Detection

University of Agriculture, Faisalabad · Final Year Project Track 2

A drone with an RTSP-capable camera streams live video of crop fields to a ground station, where a YOLOv8 model detects crops and weeds for precision agriculture. This forms the computer-vision backbone of my final year engineering work.

  • 80.65%Average crop detection confidence, cotton and maize
  • 3.1 FPSReal-time processing speed
  • 4 cropsIn the dataset: wheat, rice, cotton, maize
  • Receives the RTSP video stream with OpenCV, then resizes, normalizes and enhances frames before detection.
  • Trained on drone footage of wheat, rice, cotton and maize, split 70% training, 15% validation and 15% testing, collected in different light, times of day and growth stages.
  • Recognizes irrigated areas such as water channels, moist soil and irrigation furrows, giving a field-wide view in place of point readings.
  • Includes a Flask web dashboard for live video, detection results and reports.
  • Field-tested on cotton and maize. Weed detection averaged 83.7% confidence in cotton and 29.3% in maize, where grass-type weeds resemble the crop. Wheat and rice are in the training data but were out of season, so they were not validated.
